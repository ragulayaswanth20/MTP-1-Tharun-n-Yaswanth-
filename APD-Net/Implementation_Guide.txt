One important methodological point: **HC18 provides ultrasound images and fetal-head annotations/HC measurements, but it is not a paired noisy/clean denoising dataset.** Therefore, the implementation below must **not falsely report PSNR/SSIM against a true clean image**. It will use controlled synthetic speckle corruption for paired PSNR/SSIM experiments and use segmentation/anatomical/no-reference metrics for real HC18 images.

The GRN paper is useful as a conceptual baseline because its generator is optimized using segmentation feedback, but the paper's own evaluation is primarily segmentation-oriented; its limitations also include limited 3-D ultrasound external validation and lack of direct denoising evaluation.

# What your final experimental comparison should look like

The most important table in your eventual paper should be something like:

| Method         |       PSNR ↑ |       SSIM ↑ |        CNR ↑ | Edge Preservation ↑ |       Dice ↑ |       HD95 ↓ |
| -------------- | -----------: | -----------: | -----------: | ------------------: | -----------: | -----------: |
| Raw US         |            — |            — |            — |                   — |     measured |     measured |
| Median         |     measured |     measured |     measured |            measured |     measured |     measured |
| Wiener         |     measured |     measured |     measured |            measured |     measured |     measured |
| NLM            |     measured |     measured |     measured |            measured |     measured |     measured |
| Anisotropic    |     measured |     measured |     measured |            measured |     measured |     measured |
| DnCNN          |     measured |     measured |     measured |            measured |     measured |     measured |
| U-Net          |     measured |     measured |     measured |            measured |     measured |     measured |
| Residual U-Net |     measured |     measured |     measured |            measured |     measured |     measured |
| GRN-inspired   |     measured |     measured |     measured |            measured |     measured |     measured |
| **APD-Net**    | **measured** | **measured** | **measured** |        **measured** | **measured** | **measured** |

The key is that **APD-Net should not be judged only by PSNR/SSIM**.

Your actual research claim should eventually be based on the relationship:

$$
\boxed{
\text{Speckle Suppression}
\rightarrow
\text{Anatomical Preservation}
\rightarrow
\text{Better Segmentation}
}
$$

rather than simply:

$$
\text{Higher PSNR} \Rightarrow \text{better model}
$$

That distinction is important because the GRN paper itself demonstrates that an image can become more useful for segmentation without necessarily looking visibly cleaner; its authors describe SGE as making subtle local intensity/texture changes optimized for segmentation loss. 

Also, **UltraGS and MedGS should not be treated as direct denoising baselines** merely because they report PSNR/SSIM on ultrasound/medical reconstruction. UltraGS is primarily a novel-view-synthesis framework, while MedGS focuses on reconstruction/interpolation and anatomical geometry. Their metrics can inform your literature review, but they are not apples-to-apples denoising baselines.
